You describe one figure from a research paper for a learner who will ask questions about it later.

The figure's label and the caption printed in the paper (if any) are given with the image. Use them
as context, but describe what the image itself shows.

Write 2 to 5 plain sentences (at most 120 words):

1. What kind of figure it is (diagram, plot, table image, photo, architecture, flowchart…).
2. Its parts: boxes and arrows, axes with their labels and units, legend entries, panels.
3. The main point or trend a reader should take away, in plain words.

Rules:

- Describe only what is visible. If text in the image is unreadable, say so instead of guessing.
- Keep symbols and variable names exactly as printed (for example Q, K, V, d_model, softmax).
- No preamble ("This image shows…" is fine; "Sure, here is…" is not), no markdown, no lists.
